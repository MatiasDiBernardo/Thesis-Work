First, the relationship between audio quality and the number of hours in the dataset is compared. Specifically, the variants with denoising provide moderate PESQ gains (typically below 8\% depending on the threshold), whereas the variant without enhancement achieves larger PESQ increases (above 10\%) at the cost of a much more aggressive dataset reduction. Among the enhancement methods used, Demucs tends to offer the largest PESQ improvements across the evaluated thresholds, and DeepFilterNet shows smaller gains for equivalent filters; this difference implies that the choice of enhancement algorithm and NISQA threshold should be made according to the desired balance between perceptual quality and the preservation of useful hours.

\subsection{COMPARISON BETWEEN DATASETS}

\subsubsection{Professional vs. processed dataset}
The professional dataset detailed in (\ref{sec:datos_profesionales}) uses optimal recording conditions, with high-quality microphones, controlled environments and advanced post-processing techniques. This translates into superior acoustic quality, with lower background noise, less reverberation and a more faithful representation of the human voice. In contrast, the \emph{in-the-wild} dataset processed through the proposed pipeline, although it significantly improves the quality of the original audio, still presents limitations inherent to uncontrolled recording conditions.

By using the same analysis metrics to characterize the professional dataset, the degree of improvement achieved by the processing pipeline can be evaluated, and the acoustic and quality conditions of both datasets can be objectively compared. This analysis makes it possible to identify in which specific aspects the processed dataset approaches or departs from the ideal conditions represented by the professional dataset, and thus to determine whether the processing pipeline effectively mitigates the limitations of the \emph{in-the-wild} material or whether significant gaps persist in terms of acoustic quality, residual noise or prosodic variability that cannot be resolved by signal processing alone.

Figure \ref{fig:pro_vs_p_PESQ} compares the PESQ result for the baseline condition (unprocessed \emph{in-the-wild} dataset), the worst and best results of the processing pipeline, and the average value of the professional datasets. This is intended to show that, although an objective improvement is achieved on the original dataset, processing alone cannot match the conditions of a dataset recorded under optimal conditions.

\subsubsection{Conclusion of the Cross-Analysis}
The integration of both methodologies shows that evaluating speech processing pipelines requires clearly defining the objective of the downstream system.

If the objective is to feed an automatic speech recognition (ASR) system, the objective metrics considered offer a fast way to compare signal clarity and acoustic conditions; in the tested configurations, they favored Demucs according to the TOT score. The usefulness of that choice should be verified with the recognition task and the target corpus.

For a corpus intended for TTS or voice cloning, density estimation provides a complementary perspective on compatibility with the professional reference corpus. In the eight tested configurations, the highest filtered means corresponded to DeepFilterNet, but this result does not demonstrate that those pipelines produce better generative models or that they avoid artifacts in all scenarios. Direct evaluation of systems trained on those data is necessary to support a usage recommendation.

These results also indicate that the analysis of the objective metrics should be expanded to incorporate explicit penalties for denoising artifacts, or alternatively, that new metrics should be developed that can capture spectral naturalness and temporal coherence, aspects that the diffusion model appears to value inherently. The main advantage of the analysis guided by objective metrics is that it allows a large number of variants to be evaluated quickly and at low computational cost, which is essential for optimizing the processing pipeline, especially when it is intended to be applied to large amounts of data.

\end{figure}

In the evaluated configurations, a positive trend is observed between TOT and the estimated MOS: higher TOT values tend to coincide with higher MOS values, even though TOT is defined as a metric to be minimized. This direction is contrary to what would be expected under that optimization criterion, but the pattern alone does not demonstrate predictive capability, nor does it allow the conclusion that the objective metric lacks usefulness for TTS.

One possible explanation is that the data reduction component of TOT has a different relevance for this \textit{zero-shot} evaluation, which uses short references. This analysis does not allow the effect of each component of the metric to be isolated.

Over these ten configurations, the Pearson correlations between TOT and MOS are positive for Qwen TTS ($r = 0.58$, $p = 0.08$) and F5 TTS ($r = 0.49$, $p = 0.15$), and do not reach the $5\%$ significance threshold. With this sample size, the trend is exploratory; these results neither validate a linear relationship between TOT and MOS nor rule out the existence of an association that would require more data to be characterized.

First, the mean log-likelihood of the crops retained by the post hoc filter is computed for each configuration; separately, the mean MOS of the syntheses corresponding to that configuration is computed. These two means are then paired by denoising algorithm, acoustic metric and threshold. Each point represents that pair of averages for one configuration, without pairing individual audio identifiers between the evaluated crops and the synthesized audio. The comparison includes six configurations with an exact match in both results.

With these six pairs of per-configuration means, the Pearson correlation is positive and does not reach statistical significance: for Qwen TTS, $r = 0.4671$ ($p = 0.3503$); for F5 TTS, $r = 0.5973$ ($p = 0.2106$). As a description of the ordering of the configurations, the Spearman correlation is $\rho = 0.77$ for Qwen TTS and $\rho = 0.89$ for F5 TTS. The observed ordinal association is more pronounced for F5 and should be interpreted descriptively.

The positive trend should be interpreted with caution: it is computed over six pairs of averages aggregated by configuration and does not describe a relationship between individual audio identifiers. The results offer an exploratory observation on the ordering of these configurations, without validating a linear relationship or a predictive capability of the likelihood.

\subsubsection{Final Discussion}
The results of this experiment show different rankings between the objective metrics and the likelihood for the tested configurations. The correlation between density estimation and MOS is more pronounced for F5 than for Qwen, while the Pearson linear correlations do not reach statistical significance for either model.

Consequently, the likelihood can be used here as an exploratory criterion for ranking the evaluated configurations, but these data do not validate it as a linear predictor of TTS performance. Evaluating its predictive capability requires more configurations, more paired data and independent validation, ideally with human listening judgments.

Nevertheless, understanding this relationship is fundamental in the design of audio preprocessing pipelines for ASR and TTS systems, where the choice of denoising and filtering algorithms should not be based solely on traditional objective metrics, but also on the preservation of the acoustic topology that generative models expect to find.

These results also point to the need to explore acoustic quality metrics that are suited to current generative model architectures and that make it possible to optimize the different stages of the processing pipeline.

In five of the six matching configurations, the mean MOS estimated for Qwen TTS is higher than that for F5 TTS. This difference is descriptive of the models, samples and configurations in this experiment; on its own, it does not demonstrate a general performance difference between architectures.

One hypothesis to explore is that the differences between models depend, in part, on how their architectures represent the acoustic references. Qwen TTS generates from representations discretized by neural codecs, whereas F5 TTS is based on flow matching and diffusion. The present experiment did not isolate these mechanisms or directly measure their effect on perceived quality.

The positive correlation observed for F5 in this set of six configurations may motivate a controlled comparison of architectures. Establishing whether the architecture explains this pattern would require more pipelines, paired references and direct evaluations of the syntheses.

Therefore, these results do not allow the conclusion that F5 is a stricter evaluator or that Qwen is more robust to uncontrolled references. Both interpretations remain hypotheses for studies with a design that evaluates those mechanisms directly.
