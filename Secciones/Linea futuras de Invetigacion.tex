The findings and limitations identified throughout this work open up several lines of continuation aimed both at improving the processing pipeline and at refining the evaluation methodologies employed.

A first line, derived directly from the divergence observed between enhancement algorithms, consists of developing \textit{speech enhancement} models that are adaptive or tailored to the acoustic domain of the target generative model. Instead of applying general-purpose denoising, such models would seek, through domain adaptation techniques, to bring diverse recordings closer to the acoustic distribution expected by the synthesis system, preserving the spectral topology of the signal and avoiding the introduction of artifacts that the model would inevitably replicate, thereby increasing the robustness and fidelity of the resulting synthesis.

Closely related to the above, there is a need to design new low-computational-cost objective metrics capable of explicitly penalizing topological artifacts, in particular the spectral holes generated by source separation algorithms. The traditional metrics evaluated in this work proved insufficient to capture the structural integrity required by modern generative architectures, while the diffusion model, although more faithful to the perception of such systems, has a prohibitive inference cost for large-scale application. The development of descriptors that capture spectral naturalness and temporal coherence with the efficiency of classical metrics would make it possible to reconcile evaluation speed with predictive validity, a fundamental aspect for optimizing pipelines designed to process large volumes of data.

A third line of work is oriented towards scaling the collection of \textit{in-the-wild} data with the aim of exhaustively covering all the linguistic regions of Argentina. The present research validated the preprocessing pipeline on two representative dialectal variants; extending coverage to all the accents of the country would consolidate a corpus of greater cultural representativeness and would contribute to the development of regionally rooted speech technologies, in line with the objectives of technological sovereignty that motivate this work.

Likewise, given the exploratory nature of the \textit{zero-shot} synthesis experiments, it is imperative to carry out standardized subjective validations through human listening tests that would allow the observed trends to be categorically confirmed. The automated prediction of subjective quality is subject to multiple factors inherent to the synthesizers themselves; therefore, the incorporation of perceptual evaluations with formal methodologies and cross-validation would lend statistical soundness to the conclusion that density estimation constitutes a robust predictor of performance in generative tasks.

Finally, it is proposed to explore different weightings of the variables that make up the joint objective evaluation metric. The analysis of variance performed showed that certain blocks, in particular data reduction, exert a disproportionate influence on the final ranking due to the characteristics of the initial dataset. Investigating weighting schemes that balance the contribution of each component, or that prioritize specific criteria according to the objective of the \textit{downstream} system, would make it possible to adapt the selection methodology to different use cases, whether by favoring the amount of usable material, the preservation of vocal identity, or the maximization of acoustic quality.
