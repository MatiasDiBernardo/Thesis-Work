\subsection{RATIONALE}

Text-to-speech (TTS) models have undergone exponential technological progress in recent years: deep neural networks have achieved results of high sound quality and intelligibility (\cite{survey1}). However, the strong dependence of these systems on training data makes it difficult to obtain natural-sounding synthesized voices for the wide diversity of speakers, given that design decisions in the industry tend to impose a hegemonic linguistic standard regarding which voices deserve to be represented (\cite{markl2023language}). This difficulty is especially notable for regional varieties that lack specific corpora intended for TTS.

Within this framework, TTS systems in Rioplatense Spanish have been developed (\cite{sintetica}) that achieve acceptable results, but they face limited availability of data specific to the different dialects of Argentina. The gap does not consist of a general lack of data in Spanish, but rather of a scarcity of Argentine corpora intended for TTS that are large, reusable under suitable licenses, balanced across speakers, and documented by dialectal variety. Traditionally, the creation of databases for training TTS models has focused on collecting large volumes of high-quality recordings (made in professional studios) and on employing speakers with specific characteristics (for example, voice-over artists), which yields a corpus that is homogeneous in its acoustic and prosodic characteristics, but which generates numerous biases and a lack of representativeness when one seeks to adapt such systems to dialectal variants not contemplated in the initial data collection (\cite{koenecke2020racial}). The traditional data collection model was important for the convergence of deep learning-based models, but it represents a barrier to entry for numerous dialectal varieties that lack the resources to produce such datasets.

The speech processing literature refers to these cases as ``low-resource languages''; they include specific dialects of a language, such as the variants characteristic of particular Argentine provinces. To train TTS models with greater naturalness, the use of data collected from the Internet has been explored (\cite{erica}), forming heterogeneous datasets drawn from diverse sources and of variable audio quality. These corpora are commonly referred to as in-the-wild (ITW) data. Moreover, with the advance of generative artificial intelligence, various improvements have emerged in the architectures of the most recent TTS systems (\cite{survey2}), which makes ITW datasets an especially attractive source for capturing the great diversity of the speech phenomenon.

The main problem with training TTS models on ITW datasets is the high variability in recording quality, which directly affects the ability of neural models to learn the underlying patterns and, in many cases, prevents convergence towards satisfactory results. To address this limitation, preprocessing pipelines have recently been proposed that extract, from a large dataset, subsets with better audio quality (\cite{autoprep}). Although different variants of these pipelines exist in the literature, no exhaustive acoustic characterization has been carried out of the variability exhibited by the resulting datasets after their application. Validation is usually based on training TTS models and evaluating their convergence; however, the entire pipeline is not usually characterized by means of acoustic parameters that would allow different implementations to be compared under common criteria, nor are optimal configurations defined according to different objectives (for example, maximizing audio quality versus maximizing the number of hours in the corpus). The impact of data quality on the training of TTS models has been studied in depth (\cite{improv_tts2}), but the differences between ITW datasets and professional datasets have not been analyzed through an objective analysis.

The research proposed in this thesis aims to determine the effectiveness of these processing pipelines by means of acoustic parameters. This type of analysis not only facilitates the iteration and optimization of speech audio filtering processes, but also opens up the possibility of more easily developing databases for low-resource languages, thereby contributing to the availability of higher-quality TTS systems for a wide variety of languages and local accents.


\subsection{OBJECTIVES}

\subsubsection{General objective}
The objective of this research is to evaluate, using objective and subjective parameters, the impact of \emph{in-the-wild} dataset processing pipelines on the training of text-to-speech models based on deep neural networks.

\subsubsection{Specific objectives}

The specific objectives are:

\begin{itemize}
    \item Create an \emph{in-the-wild} dataset in Argentine Spanish. Collect datasets of professional voices in Spanish (high-quality recordings made by professional speakers).
    \item Develop a modular automatic preprocessing pipeline for the generation of speech datasets, and process the ITW dataset with the pipeline under different operating configurations.
    \item Evaluate acoustic metrics on the different generated datasets, compare these results with those obtained on traditional datasets, and determine, according to acoustic criteria, which of the generated datasets can be considered optimal (comparing the mean and standard deviation of the different datasets).
    \item Train a distribution estimation model and compare the similarity between the different datasets in the latent space. Determine the optimal dataset according to similarity criteria based on density estimation.
    \item Compare the results of the acoustic analysis with those derived from the density estimation analysis. Statistically analyze the relevance of the differences observed in the various parameters.
    \item Validate the results in the context of voice cloning using zero-shot TTS models.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{STRUCTURE OF THE RESEARCH}

This thesis corresponds to a technological research project aimed at the development and evaluation of a software tool for the automatic selection of audio, intended for the generation of speech datasets. Validating this type of tool, prioritizing low computational cost and the use of \textit{in-the-wild} data, is fundamental to democratizing the creation of acoustic corpora in low-resource languages. By enabling the autonomous construction of a dataset representative of the diverse accents of Argentina, structural dependence on foreign technology corporations, which have historically dictated the standards of linguistic representation (\cite{ricaurte2019data}), is reduced. In this sense, the developed tool contributes to the advancement of speech technologies in the country and lays concrete foundations towards national technological sovereignty, understood here as the inalienable capacity of local institutions to develop, control, and manage their own technologies of cultural and communicational representation.

The development of this thesis is part of the Archivoz project of the research group Intercambios Transorgánicos, founded and led by Lic. Gala Lucia Gonzalez Barrios, and based since 2015 at the Muntref Centro de Arte y Ciencia. Within the research group, the Archivoz project began in 2019 with the aim of formulating questions and proposals for the development of speech technologies, mainly focused on patients who have undergone a laryngectomy as part of survival treatment for laryngeal cancer. A laryngectomy consists of the removal of the larynx, and with it the vocal folds. Laryngectomized individuals may therefore retain the motor and cognitive capacity for phonation, but, lacking vocal folds, they cannot produce the sound of the laryngeal voice (\cite{gala}).

One of the proposals of this research is the development of TTS systems that allow patients to regain the ability to communicate through the synthesis of their own voice, using voice cloning models and deep learning techniques for this purpose.
