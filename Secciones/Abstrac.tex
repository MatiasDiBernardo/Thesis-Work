\begin{centering}
\Large{\textbf{ABSTRACT}}

\end{centering}

Text-to-speech systems require abundant, high-quality training data, which is a challenge for languages and dialectal varieties such as Argentine Spanish. Datasets collected from heterogeneous sources offer an alternative but require preprocessing. This study evaluates the effects of different processing pipelines using objective and subjective measures and examines their impact on data intended for TTS training.

A modular, reproducible pipeline combining voice activity detection, signal enhancement, and perceptual filtering was developed. DeepFilterNet and Demucs, NISQA and DNSMOS, and different thresholds were compared. The composite metric considers corpus reduction, signal quality and acoustic conditions, and preservation of speaker characteristics. All likelihood results exclude individual crops with log-likelihood values greater than 4.0. In addition, a diffusion-based density model was trained on a professional reference corpus, and voice cloning with zero-shot synthesis models was evaluated exploratorily.

The criteria produced different rankings: Demucs with DNSMOS 2.7 achieved the best acoustic result, while DeepFilterNet had the highest mean likelihood values (2.37 and 2.30). Exploratorily, this suggests that more aggressive noise suppression favors the acoustic metrics, whereas conservative processing preserves structures valued by the density model; eight configurations are insufficient to confirm or generalize this interpretation. For TTS, means from six matched configurations were compared. The association between likelihood and MOS was descriptively stronger for F5 than for Qwen, suggesting possible practical utility. These results do not validate the metrics as predictors of quality. Better descriptors are needed to study interactions among processing stages, particularly those based on deep neural networks.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} speech synthesis, speech datasets, audio preprocessing, low-resource languages
