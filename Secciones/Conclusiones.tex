The present work addressed the evaluation of preprocessing pipelines for generating speech datasets from \textit{in-the-wild} material, by means of objective acoustic metrics and density estimation with probabilistic diffusion models. For the likelihood results, the canonical series filtered post hoc at the crop level was used: values greater than $4.0$ were excluded and those equal to or lower were retained. The comparison between criteria is limited to the evaluated configurations and shows that each one summarizes different aspects of the processing.

The rankings by TOT and by filtered likelihood differed within the eight configurations with results in both analyses. The Demucs configuration with DNSMOS 2.7 obtained the lowest TOT (4.08), whereas the highest mean likelihoods corresponded to DeepFilterNet with DNSMOS 2.7 (2.37) and NISQA 3.8 (2.30); the four Demucs configurations fell between 1.69 and 1.88. This pattern suggests that the chosen criterion may change the priority assigned to a pipeline. The spectral differences observed in the Demucs example offer a possible explanation, although they do not demonstrate the mechanism nor establish that a single criterion by itself represents perceptual quality or TTS performance.

In the exploratory TTS evaluation, mean filtered likelihood and MOS were compared for six matching configurations. The Pearson linear correlations between likelihood and MOS were positive but not significant: Qwen TTS obtained $r = 0.4671$ ($p = 0.3503$) and F5 TTS, $r = 0.5973$ ($p = 0.2106$). The ordinal likelihood-MOS association was more marked for F5 ($\rho = 0.89$) than for Qwen ($\rho = 0.77$), descriptively and with $n=6$. In a separate comparison of TOT and MOS, based on ten matching configurations, the correlations were also positive and did not reach significance (Qwen: $r = 0.58$, $p = 0.08$; F5: $r = 0.49$, $p = 0.15$). These data do not validate likelihood as a linear predictor of TTS performance. The trend requires evaluation with more configurations, more paired data, and independent validation.

In five of the six matching configurations, Qwen TTS obtained a higher estimated mean MOS than F5 TTS. This difference and the somewhat more marked ordinal association for F5 may guide further research on the role of architecture. The experiment did not isolate the mechanisms of each model, nor does it allow concluding that one is generally more robust or more sensitive to artifacts.

Finally, in the compared corpora, the \textit{in-the-wild} dataset showed greater prosodic variability, measured by the standard deviation of the fundamental frequency. This result supports exploring the combination of professional data and data collected under real-world conditions to broaden prosodic and dialectal variety; the study does not by itself prove that such incorporation improves the generalization of all models.
