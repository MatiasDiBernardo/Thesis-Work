\subsection{TTS MODELS}
\label{sec:estado_arte_tts}
The development of neural network-based speech synthesis systems has undergone a rapid evolution, moving from complex sequential models towards probabilistic generative architectures. The technological progression of these systems is described below, classified according to their generation paradigm.

\subsubsection{Autoregressive Models}
The first generation of high-fidelity neural models was based on the autoregressive paradigm, in which the generation of each segment of the mel spectrogram depends on the previously generated segment.

The most representative model of this stage is Tacotron 2 (\cite{shen2018natural}). This architecture combines a recurrent network (LSTM) with an attention mechanism to map the character sequence to a Mel spectrogram, which is subsequently converted into a waveform by a vocoder (originally WaveNet). Although Tacotron 2 set a new standard of naturalness, its attention mechanism is prone to alignment errors, resulting in robustness problems such as word repetition or omission.

To mitigate the robustness failures of the attention mechanism, DurIAN (Duration Informed Attention Network) emerged (\cite{yu2019durian}). This model replaces the traditional attention mechanism with an alignment model that uses phoneme durations (predicted or extracted through forced alignment). Although DurIAN still generates acoustic frames autoregressively, the incorporation of explicit duration information significantly improves synthesis stability, eliminating a large part of the artifacts present in Tacotron 2.

\subsubsection{Non-Autoregressive and Parallel Models}
The main limitation of autoregressive models is their slow inference speed, since generation cannot be parallelized. To solve this, architectures capable of generating the complete spectrogram in a single step emerged.

FastSpeech 2 (\cite{ren2020fastspeech}) marked a milestone in this category by proposing a fully "\textit{feed-forward}" network based on transformer blocks. Unlike its predecessors, FastSpeech 2 trains explicit variance predictors (duration, energy, and fundamental frequency $F_0$) directly on the text input. This not only enables extremely fast (parallel) inference, but also provides greater control over prosody and greater robustness to noisy data.

Subsequently, the VITS model (Variational Inference with adversarial learning for end-to-end Text-to-Speech) (\cite{kim2021conditional}) proposed a unified approach. VITS combines normalizing flows and a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) architecture with adversarial training (GAN). Unlike two-stage systems (acoustic model + vocoder), VITS is a complete system that directly generates the waveform, achieving state-of-the-art quality with naturalness superior to previous two-stage models.

\subsubsection{Diffusion and Flow Matching-based Models}
With the rise of Generative Artificial Intelligence, models based on probabilistic diffusion (DDPM) and, more recently, the \textit{Flow Matching} paradigm have demonstrated superior capabilities for modeling the complex distribution of the human voice. These architectures stand out particularly in speaker adaptation and \textit{zero-shot} synthesis tasks, that is, the ability of the model to generate the voice of an unseen speaker from an extremely short acoustic reference (generally a few seconds long), without requiring any kind of retraining or \textit{fine-tuning} of its parameters.

Diff-TTS (\cite{jeong2021diff}) was one of the pioneers in applying probabilistic diffusion models to the estimation of Mel spectrograms. This model formulates synthesis as an iterative refinement process, transforming Gaussian noise into a speech signal conditioned on the text. Although the quality is high, the computational cost of the multiple sampling steps is considerable.

To address the latency inherent to diffusion, Grad-TTS (\cite{popov2021grad}) introduced a framework based on stochastic differential equations (SDE) with strict monotonic alignment, allowing a flexible trade-off between speed and quality. Along the same line of optimization, ProDiff (\cite{huang2022prodiff}) employs progressive distillation techniques to drastically reduce the number of required sampling steps, enabling high-quality synthesis in as few as 2 or 4 diffusion steps.

Finally, the state of the art has converged towards the \textit{Flow Matching} paradigm, a generalization of diffusion models that learns vector fields to transform simple distributions into complex data distributions through straighter and more efficient trajectories. The F5-TTS model (\cite{chen2024f5}) represents the current culmination of this trend. F5-TTS uses an architecture based on \textit{Diffusion Transformers} (DiT) with a \textit{Flow Matching} training scheme. Unlike previous models that require complex alignments or explicit duration predictors, F5-TTS is trained with a binary masking system (it masks random segments of the audio and lets the model learn to predict that segment), enabling \textit{zero-shot} voice cloning and emotion control capabilities with unprecedented naturalness and competitive inference speed thanks to the efficiency of Flow Matching.
